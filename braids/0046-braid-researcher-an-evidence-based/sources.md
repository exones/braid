All accessed 2026-11-20. "Read" says how much of the source was seen verbatim. Round 1 (S1–S10) was read through web-fetch subagents that returned quotes and summaries. In round 2 (S11–S26), PDFs that the fetcher could not decode were extracted locally with `pdftotext`, and two PMC articles were taken as full-text XML from Europe PMC; the worker read those texts directly. ## Round 2

- S11 Richards J. Heuer Jr., Psychology of Intelligence Analysis (CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence) — https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf · version: 1999 edition · read: part (ch. 4 from "Strategies for Choice Among Hypotheses" to the end, ch. 5 first half including the handicapper experiment, ch. 8 whole, ch. 10 to "Persistence of Impressions") · says: most analysis is "satisficing", picking the first good-enough hypothesis, which brings selective perception, too few hypotheses and confirming instead of refuting; people "are actually rather poor at thinking of all the possibilities"; in Slovic's handicapper experiment accuracy stayed flat from 5 to 40 variables while confidence kept rising, and "once an experienced analyst has the minimum information necessary... obtaining additional information generally does not improve the accuracy... [but] lead[s] the analyst to become more confident"; medical students who stressed thorough data collection over forming and testing hypotheses diagnosed worse; ACH step 6: question the "linchpin assumptions", "go back to check original source materials rather than relying on someone else's interpretation"; step 7: report the relative likelihood of all hypotheses; step 8: specify in advance what would change your mind; "Whenever an intelligence analyst is tempted to write the phrase 'there is no evidence that...', [ask] can I realistically expect to see evidence of it?"; ch. 10: vivid anecdotes outweigh statistics, absence of evidence is "out of sight, out of mind", consistency from "highly correlated or redundant" or small samples gives an "illusion of validity", and impressions persist after their evidence is discredited. Heuer says ACH "increases the odds" but that there is "no guarantee". · adopted: competing candidates in the frame (1), hypothesis-led search and stopping (1, 10), linchpin assumptions (7), disconfirm and "no evidence that" (6), independence (5), what would change the answer (report). Not adopted: the full matrix (see S15).
- S12 A Tradecraft Primer: Structured Analytic Techniques for Improving Intelligence Analysis (US Government) — https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf · version: March 2009 · read: part (introduction, How to Use, Key Assumptions Check, Quality of Information Check, Devil's Advocacy, Strategies for Using) · says: Key Assumptions Check is "most useful at the beginning of an analytic project"; "Having multiple sources on an issue is not a substitute for having good information that has been thoroughly examined"; quoted WMD commission: "over-reliance on a single, ambiguous source"; the techniques are "no guarantee of analytic precision or accuracy". · adopted: assumptions in the frame (1), judge sources within a rank (3), independence over count (5). Not adopted: team techniques (Team A/B, red team); a single agent can't run them.
- S13 ICD 203 Analytic Standards (ODNI) — https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf (served from archive.dni.gov) · version: signed 2015-01-02, technical amendment signed 2022-01-21 · read: whole · says: describe "quality and credibility of underlying sources", including "age and continued currency of information" and "possible bias"; express likelihood with one fixed set of terms ("almost no chance" 01–05% to "almost certain(ly)" 95–99%) and never combine a confidence level and a likelihood "in the same sentence"; "clearly distinguish statements that convey underlying intelligence information... from statements that convey assumptions or judgments"; state linchpin assumptions and "the implications for judgments if assumptions prove to be incorrect"; "identify and assess plausible alternative hypotheses"; "present a clear main analytic message up front"; "should not avoid difficult judgments in order to minimize the risk of being wrong". · adopted: likelihood scale and separate confidence (report), labels and assumptions (7), alternatives (6), answer first (report), version and currency (9).
- S14 Chang, Chen, Mellers, Tetlock, "Developing expert political judgment: The impact of training and practice on judgmental accuracy in geopolitical forecasting tournaments", Judgment and Decision Making 11(5) — https://journal.sjdm.org/16/16511/jdm16511.html (redirects to jbaron.org) · version: 2016 · read: part (about the first half verbatim, through section 2.4 and Tables 5–7; the rest only as a subagent summary) · says: under an hour of probabilistic-reasoning training (base rates, updating on "the probative value of new evidence", averaging, calibration) "consistently improved accuracy (Brier scores) by 6 to 11%" across four tournament years; self-reported use of comparison classes had the strongest link to accuracy; the authors call the component analysis correlational. · adopted: base rate first (2). Not adopted: numeric Brier scoring; a single question gives no feedback to score.
- S15 Mandel, Karvetski, Dhami, "Boosting intelligence analysts' judgment accuracy: What works, what fails?", Judgment and Decision Making 13(6) — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/boosting-intelligence-analysts-judgment-accuracy-what-works-what-fails/1530E9DAE8F42B2E8FC2B5FE374DB50F · version: November 2018 · read: part (abstract and introduction verbatim; method and results only as a subagent summary) · says: 50 UK analysts randomly assigned to ACH or no method; "ACH failed to improve accuracy" and "the control group was a little more accurate (and coherent)"; "there is little scientific research on ACH"; ACH "does nothing to ensure that analysts consider prior probabilities or objective base rates"; aggregation after coherentizing cut error by 61%. · adopted: evidence against a heavy ACH procedure; keep only the cheap parts (candidates, disconfirm) and add base rates (2). Not adopted: aggregation of several judges; one agent has one judgment.
